    readme.txt

    I Love My Computer, on a simulated record player

    A fan remix of Guillermo Rauch's vinyl, a record player simulated from the groove up, rebuilt for Ninajirachi's album I Love My Computer.

    Bring your own copy

    No music from the album comes with this page. Insert your copy of Fuck My Computer and your browser cuts it into a spiral groove, presses a record and plays it with a simulated stylus. The file never leaves your computer. When the page recognises the song, the room performs it: her lyrics, her vocal chops, the build-ups and the drops. You can buy the album on Bandcamp.

    The deck

    Nothing here is an audio player. The lathe turns the music into the two walls of a stereo groove, millions of points each. The pressing plant adds dust, scratches and warp. The engine moves a stylus against both walls hundreds of thousands of times a second, and what you hear is the voltage a moving-magnet cartridge would make. Click the record to drop the needle anywhere, drag it to scratch, scroll to zoom in on the stylus. groove.exe, bottom left, shows the groove walls passing under the tip.

    Flashing

    The room strobes and flashes in time with the music. Flashing off (top right, or the L key) keeps the show but drops the strobes, the flashes and the glitches.

    Credits

    Music, lyrics and the :3 face: Ninajirachi. Deck, groove and physics: Guillermo Rauch (MIT). The room: Dara Adedeji. The records on the shelf are public domain or freely licensed, each credited on the shelf. Built with three.js.

    An unofficial fan project, not affiliated with Ninajirachi or her label. Source on GitHub.
